Bocas del Toro

An eco-lodge and wildlife sanctuary on Isla Popa, where the rainforest meets mangrove and reef. Poison frogs, tropical birds, kayaking and snorkeling, all in one place.

The lodge was built from local materials by local hands, and serves as a base for wildlife observation and conservation projects. Every stay includes one nature experience per day —from birding at dawn to the forest after dark— and part of every reservation funds scientific and community work across the archipelago.

Defining wildlife See all wildlife
  • Amphibian

    Dink frog

    Diasporus sp.

    Tiny and high-pitched, it shows up at night among the leaf litter of the Bocas forest. You hear it long before you see it.
  • Amphibian

    Red-eyed tree frog

    Agalychnis callidryas

    The most iconic frog of the American tropics. It shows up at night on leaves above the forest pools.
  • Mammal

    Sloth

    Bradypus sp.

    A calm dweller of the Bocas canopy. With a sharp eye and a guide, it turns up hanging from the trail branches.

San Blas mountains · cloud mountain

San Blas Mountain

A mountain eco-lodge wrapped in continuous forest, home of the Limosa harlequin toad. These are not the San Blas islands or beaches: this is the cloud mountain, on the road to Guna Yala.
Field data
Region
San Blas mountains · cloud mountain
Getting there
In the San Blas mountains, about 2 hours from Panama City. A 4x4 vehicle is recommended. Exact directions are shared once your booking is confirmed.
Coordinates
9.30° N · 79.00° WApproximate coordinates. We send the exact route when you book.
Lodging
6 private cabins + 1 dorm · from USD $25

Konsenda San Blas began as an amphibian monitoring station and grew into a refuge for naturalists. The cabins are named after the species around them —Tucán, Atelopus, Trogón, Sapayoa— and the trails link hummingbird feeders, lookouts and the river waterfall. At night the forest comes alive: time to go frog-hunting with the biologists.

Defining wildlife See all wildlife
  • Amphibian · emblem

    Limosa harlequin toad

    Atelopus limosus

    Critically endangered. It survives in a few of the country’s mountain forests, where it is monitored season after season.
  • Amphibian

    Glass frog

    Hyalinobatrachium sp.

    Its transparent belly reveals the heart and organs. It appears on leaves over the streams during night searches.
  • Bird

    White-necked jacobin

    Florisuga mellivora

    Visits the hummingbird feeders right outside the cabins. A photographer favorite for its blue hood.
